Turnout vs expectations

is the electorate that has shown up more or less Democratic than the expectation implies?
RaceElectorateBaselineExpected swing Expected from turnoutTurnout effect seen vs pollsrangevs markets

How: a party's margin = how many of its registered voters turn out × how they vote. The turnout effect seen values the change in the electorate's party shares (registered D, R, other vs the baseline year) at typical vote margins (registered Democrats ≈ D+85, Republicans ≈ R+88, others at the lean that reproduces the baseline year's result). The expected turnout effect is the share of the expected swing that normally comes from turnout (assumed ⅓; range 20–50%) times the swing polls or markets expect. vs polls = seen − expected: D+ means turnout is running better for Democrats than the expectation implies. Florida uses all voters (early + Election Day check-ins, with the rest of Election Day finished at the baseline year's volume at today's mix); North Carolina uses early voters only, against the expected early-vote margin. 2022 is the main baseline (midterm); 2024's presidential-year electorate was bigger and less partisan.

How it works

Expected county margin = 2024 presidential county margin + the statewide swing implied by the race's final polls (or the national generic ballot, D+8.7, where no race average exists).

Vote-type adjustment: early/mail and Election Day votes lean differently and post in different orders. Each county's final margin is estimated by filling in the vote types not yet counted, using 2022 county vote-type data (or state research) and each county's actual early-ballot count.

Turnout vs expectations: the change in who voted (shares of registered Democrats, Republicans and others vs 2022) is valued at typical vote margins to get the turnout effect seen, in points of margin. If polls/markets are right, turnout should supply about a third of the swing they expect vs 2022 (assumption; 20–50% shown as a range). Seen minus expected = turnout vs expectations. Example, Florida Governor: polls expect R+3.9 vs R+19.4 in 2022, a D+15.4 swing, so turnout "should" be worth ≈ D+5.1; the electorate's shift is worth D+1.8, so turnout is running ≈ R+3.3 vs the polls.

County mix: counties not yet counted are filled with the average of three estimates (the race's read so far, a county-type model and a demographic model), the combination that backtested best on 2020→22, 2016→20 and 2020→24 swings (BACKTEST.md). Expected error ≈ ±1.8 pts at 5% counted, ±1.2 at 10%, ±0.6 at 25%.
